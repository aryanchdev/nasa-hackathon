
**100% Must-Have Basic Vitals** -

- **Blood Pressure & Heart Rate:** Blood pools in the chest in space, making the heart lazy over time and causing a high risk of fainting when landing back in gravity.
    
- **Heart Rhythm (ECG):** Space radiation and fluid shifts can trigger irregular heartbeats during heavy workouts or spacewalks.
    
- **Oxygen & Breathing Rate:** Fast breathing or low blood oxygen warns of Moon dust in the lungs or too much carbon dioxide in the cabin air.
    
- **Body Temperature:** Sweat sticks to the skin in zero gravity instead of dripping off, so astronauts run a natural "space fever" during exercise, while higher spikes warn of infection.
    
- **Blood Sugar:** Zero gravity makes the body slightly resistant to insulin, which messes with energy levels and causes fatigue.
    
- **Sleep Tracking:** NASA wrist sensors measure three exact things: how many minutes it takes to fall asleep, how many times they wake up, and total time spent awake at night.
    
- **Mood & Stress:** Tracks how isolation and tight schedules affect crew morale and teamwork.
    

**The 7 Real Body Areas NASA Studies 

- **Brain & Reaction Speed:** Instead of just asking how astronauts feel, NASA uses 10 short brain games (testing memory, reaction speed, and emotion reading) to catch "space brain fog."
    
- **Aging Blood Vessels:** Six months in space stiffens neck arteries as much as 10 to 20 years of aging on Earth, so astronauts use neck ultrasounds to check blood flow and clot risks.
    
- **Blood & Urine Chemistry:** Urine and blood tests catch hidden problems like red blood cell loss (space anemia), high cortisol stress hormones, and dissolved bone calcium that causes kidney stones.
    
- **Immune System & Saliva:** Stress and radiation weaken white blood cells, waking up dormant viruses (like shingles or cold sores) that show up in saliva before the astronaut even feels sick.
    
- **Muscle & Bone Strength:** Bone scans and leg-pull strength tests check if their 2.5-hour daily gym workout is actually protecting their legs and spine.
    
- **Inner Ear & Balance:** Floating confuses the inner ear balance system, making it hard for astronauts to walk straight or stand without falling when they land on Mars or the Moon.
    
- **Gut Bacteria:** Eating only packaged pouch food inside a sealed metal cabin changes stomach bacteria, which affects digestion, nutrition, and immunity.

**3 Points to be highlighted

- ==🔴**NASA’s "LxC" Risk Formula:** NASA grades every health threat using **Likelihood times Consequences** (how likely it happens multiplied by how badly it hurts the mission).==

  **Example A: Space Motion Sickness**
    - **Likelihood:** **5** (Nearly every astronaut gets it in the first few days).
    - **Consequence:** **1** (It passes quickly and just causes mild discomfort).
    - **Score:** 5 × 1 = **5 (Low / Green Risk)** 🟢
        
  **Example B: Solar Radiation Blast**
    - **Likelihood:** **2** (Rarely happens during a short mission).
    - **Consequence:** **5** (Fatal or causes severe, mission-ending cancer).
    - **Score:** 2 × 5 = **10 (High / Red Risk)** 🔴
    
- **Pre-Landing Salt & Water Loading:** Astronauts lose up to 20% of their blood volume in space, so NASA makes them take salt tablets and drink extra water right before landing so they do not faint when standing up.
    
- **Top Shared Priorities:** NASA links daily vitals directly with **SANS** (blurry vision from head fluid pressure) and **Radiation Dosimetry** to get a full picture of astronaut health.
